Pencil or Pickle?

An illustrated introduction to AI, presented as a read-and-listen experience with chapter navigation, page-by-page narration, adjustable reading speed, and narrated EPUB downloads.

Read & listen ↗Reader source ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

The learning purpose

Use a shared story to begin conversations about data, sensing, mistakes, and the human decisions behind AI.

Design choices

Readers control when narration begins and how quickly it plays. Chapter navigation offers manageable entry points; automatic reading pauses for chapter activities. Downloadable editions provide another way to use the resource.

My role & collaboration

Book coauthor; the linked digital reader is maintained in my GitHub portfolio. The book is collaborative work, not a sole-authored publication.

Evaluation focus

The reader demonstrates the design of a learning resource. Its features are not, by themselves, evidence of improved AI understanding.

Tube School robotics playground

A browser-based robotics sequence that moves from joint controls and action planning to an assisted demonstration, a simple imitation model, and tests of what changes when the environment changes.

Explore the simulation ↗Source & educator guide ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

Audience & purpose

Beginners and K–12 learners investigating robotic movement, examples, model fitting, and the limits of generalization.

Design choices

Eight lessons pair a goal and worked example with a learner action, optional hint, and explanatory feedback. Learners can compare the original scene with changed conditions and use an observation–reasoning–limits rubric.

Technical scope

The simulation uses browser-based MuJoCo physics and a small fitted joint-command model. It is not a general-purpose robot policy, reinforcement-learning system, or physical-robot deployment.

Attribution & evidence

An independent educational project inspired by an NVIDIA learning activity, not an NVIDIA product. Robot assets: Google DeepMind's MuJoCo Menagerie. Repository checks document simulation behavior, not measured student learning gains.

Compose and Code (CoCo)

A collaborative platform and curriculum connecting written ideas with Scratch programming through planning, differentiated support, and reflection.

Explore the project ↗Related publication ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

The learning purpose

Help learners move from a writing idea to a plan for a computational creation, rather than face an unstructured blank programming workspace.

Design choices

The project pairs a graphic organizer with Scratch-block guidance, video models, audio supports, and self-monitoring. Levels vary the amount of support and independence.

My role & collaboration

Collaborative research and curriculum work; coauthor of the published platform study. The platform belongs to the DCoaD Literacies Lab's collaborative research program and its partners.

Compose with AI

A guided writing environment that helps learners evaluate generated information and decide what to use, modify, or leave out of their own science-focused compositions.

Project walkthrough ↗Visit platform ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

The learning purpose

Make the evaluation of generated information part of composing, rather than treating a fluent response as a finished answer.

Design choices

Guidance connects purpose and audience with prompting, response evaluation, information sorting, source acknowledgment, and reflection. Students carry their decisions forward into their own composition.

My role & collaboration

Co-investigator on the supporting NSF project; collaborative platform development and pilot work with the research team. The project walkthrough is publicly available; the platform may require sign-in.

AI Story Lab

A scaffolded story-planning experience in which learners develop their own robot stories and explain the robot's data, sensors, interactions, mistakes, and retraining.

Research collaboration ↗Request a walkthrough ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

The learning purpose

Connect AI concepts with the choices learners make as authors. A robot's mistake becomes a reason to explain data and consider how retraining might change its behavior.

Design choices

A step-by-step organizer separates brainstorming into manageable prompts. Students can connect a reading of Pencil or Pickle? with the stories they write themselves.

Scope & collaboration

This is a story-planning and learning resource, not a claim that AI writes the student's story. It is presented as part of my collaborative work with the research team; a direct public application link is not included here.

ML Explorer

Six browser-based modules designed for grades 3–5: data labeling, classification, representation in data, data quality, model evaluation, and the machine-learning process.

Explore modules & source ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

The learning purpose

Break unfamiliar machine-learning ideas into short activities that pair action with prediction, feedback, and reflection.

Design choices

Modules use a common sequence: introduction, explanation, pre-check, activity, results, reflection, and completion. The repository describes local progress tracking and activities without camera or microphone access.

Scope & evidence

An educational application prototype in my GitHub collection. The modules use scripted or probabilistic simulations, not externally trained models. In-app scores and badges are activity feedback, not evidence of learning effectiveness.

Sporty Bot Label Lab

An investigation-style activity in which learners inspect mislabeled sports-ball examples, correct the labels, and compare the simulated results.

Explore project & source ↗
Learning purpose & design notes

The learning purpose

Help students connect the quality of labeled examples with the behavior illustrated by a learning system.

Design choices

The activity asks for a prediction before showing results, makes errors available for investigation, and includes reflection and assessment questions.

Scope & evidence

A learning prototype in my GitHub portfolio. Its before-and-after accuracy displays are illustrative simulation outputs, not a measured model benchmark or evidence of student learning gains.

How I approach the work

The learning goal
comes first.

I connect technical possibilities with learners' needs, instructional decisions, and evidence of understanding.

01

Start with a real need.

Who is learning, what do they need to understand, and what gets in the way? Define the purpose before selecting a tool.

02

Build support into the experience.

Use examples, manageable steps, accessible representations, and opportunities to make, test, and revise ideas.

03

Ask what learners understand.

Look beyond a finished product. Seek explanations, informed revisions, and independent application—and use the evidence to improve the design.

I am Erdogan Kaya, a computing and STEM educator working at the intersection of learning, design, and emerging technologies.
